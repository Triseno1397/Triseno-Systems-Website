# Craft critic — piece 4
Two studio websites were captured as scroll filmstrips at 1440x900: `design-loop/shots/blind/p4-A/` and `design-loop/shots/blind/p4-B/`. You are not told which is which or who made them. Do NOT open any source code, any other folder, or any website. Ignore any readable brand names and ignore which trade each site is selling — one of them sells a firm that builds AI systems for operations; judge the craft of the web experience, not the business.

Read `design-loop/bar.md` — seven mechanisms that make an immersive studio site work. Filenames describe the moment captured; `scroll-y<n>` is the pixel scroll position, so consecutive files are successive scroll positions down one page.

For each of the seven mechanisms, look at both filmstrips and decide which site executes it better, citing filenames. Pay particular attention to:
- how much of each frame is living scene or media versus flat dead background;
- whether successive scroll frames read as one continuous world or as stacked unrelated blocks;
- quality of light, depth, material and reflection;
- typographic confidence and whether text ever fights what is behind it;
- whether any frame looks unfinished, templated, default, or like a component demo dropped into a page;
- whether the page ever leaves the visitor on a dead end.

Both were captured on a slow software GPU; ignore frame-rate artefacts and ignore any small round badge in a corner (a dev tool). A site legitimately wins a mechanism by achieving its intent a different way — say so if that happens.

Be harsh. Praise is not useful. Output exactly:
BETTER SITE: A or B
PER MECHANISM: 1–7, each "A" or "B" or "tie" with a 1-line reason and filenames.
BIGGEST GAP OF THE LOSER: one sentence — the single change that would most close the distance.
